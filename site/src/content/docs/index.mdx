desalpage is a governance substrate for recursive multi-agent work: agents
delegating down a reporting structure, one bounded sprint at a time —
budgets reserved before delegation, deferrals instead of silence.

## Mission

The case for multi-agent work is public and measurable — and so is its
cost. Performance scales with the number of agents you instantiate
(["More Agents Is All You Need"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05120)), and
orchestrator–worker systems outperform single agents on breadth-first
tasks ([Anthropic, "How we built our multi-agent research
system"](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).
Anthropic measures its multi-agent research system at roughly 15× the
token spend of a chat interaction, and states the condition plainly: for
economic viability, the value of the task must be high enough to pay for
the increased performance.

The swarm approach — spawn more agents whenever the answer is uncertain —
presupposes infinite budget. Real work is bounded: every delegation spends
real tokens, and when researchers annotated execution traces to ask why
multi-agent systems fail ([MAST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)), the
failure modes were organizational: ambiguous task specifications, agents
ignoring each other's input, premature termination — mirroring how human
organizations fail.

desalpage's premise is that the remedy is governance in the harness, not
more prompting. A delegating agent reserves budget before the call, the
reservation settles on return, and every outcome lands in the ledger or
surfaces as a typed deferral — nothing silently truncated. Bounded
autonomy turns recursive delegation from a reflex into a priced decision
— the only form of it that stays affordable on a metered budget.
